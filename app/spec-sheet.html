The recorded herd: what would be needed

Spec sheet v0.3, 7 November 2026. Zamai Solutions. Companion to the demo at ngombe.zamai.pro and to CATTLE_APP_SPEC.md (the product spec) and CATTLE_PROGRAMME_CONCEPT.md (the programme). Every item is marked by how far it is from working: Have (exists and runs today), Configure (exists, needs setting up), Build (software to write, known how), Collect (data that must be gathered in the field), Research (not known to work here until tested). Costs are not given; where a number appears it is marked rough. Nothing in this sheet is a commitment.

1. Ready today

ItemStatusWhat it is, and what it gives the cattle work
Postgres database with row-level securityHaveSupabase, the open-source Postgres stack, with the same row rules we already run a business and the Tonga tool on. For the cattle work it is self-hosted: on our own machine first, in a Zambian data centre when a ministry owns it; the hosted version only for the demo. One database, many small apps; each role sees its own rows. Append-only event tables, hash-stamped, as the demo describes.
WhatsApp as one front doorHaveA working line (+260 776 621 255) with inbound and outbound messaging, templates, documents and images, and a responder that reads messages and creates entries in the database from them. In the records system we run this turned out to be the most used way in: people send what they already send, a photo and a few words, and the system makes the entry and asks one question if something is missing. The barrier is as low as it gets: the sender's number is registered to one person, so the message arrives with that person's rights, no login, no password, no install. For the cattle work we offer it as an option, a proven method that makes usage natural; which door fits which place is an open question (section 6).
Offline-first web appHaveThe Chitonga tool is an installable web app that works without signal and syncs later; the cattle demo is built the same way. Android first, cheap phones.
Tonga translationHave, improvingOur own Tonga translator (zamai.pro/tonga) and a desk of Tonga speakers who check it. For the app the rule is stricter: every label and every health line comes from a speaker, not the model. A Tonga speaker is translating the demo's 300 lines now.
Speaker networkHaveTranslators and reviewers on the Zamai line (a teacher, a priest, field officers), paid per line when paid, with a working payment and balance routine.
Local computeHave, smallA local machine for models that must stay in the country (the Tonga model runs there); rented GPU time for training runs. Enough for a few thousand pictures, not for a province.
Photo quality control over WhatsAppHave, in another domainA working routine that receives photos on WhatsApp, checks them and replies, built for Yumi Milling, the maize mill in Batoka that funds this work. The same mechanics serve the standard cattle picture.
Records expertiseHaveWe build and run records systems with row rules, stamps and many apps on one database. This is not new ground.

4. To collect (data the field must produce)

ItemStatusWhat is needed
Picture pairs for weight and conditionCollectA standard picture (side, full length; from above in the crush) paired with a scale reading, the same day. Rough need: several thousand pairs across breeds, ages and seasons before a model is worth checking; tens of thousands for one worth trusting. A feedlot produces hundreds a cycle.
Picture series per animalCollectThe same animal every day for a cycle, from fixed cameras, with weights every two weeks. This gives growth, not just weight.
Carcass pairsCollectThe live picture at exit paired with cold carcass weight, grade and condemnations from the abattoir. Needs the abattoir's data, not just a camera.
Health events with picturesCollectSick reports with signs, pictures and the vet's outcome. Thousands before any model; useful as a record from the first one.
Muzzle picturesCollect, ResearchTwo pictures a week apart of the same known animals, hundreds of animals, to test matching here (see 6).
Movement historyCollectFrom the first day the entries exist. No model needed.
PricesCollectAuction lists are public; members' sales need consent.
Standard picture protocolBuild then CollectDistance, angle, marker for scale, light; a one-page sheet and a phone overlay. Without it the pairs are noise.

5b. Hardware: three options by available funds, and what we suggest

Prices are rough estimates in US dollars at November 2026 list prices from memory, before import duty, transport and VAT, and must be checked before any purchase. Kwacha at about K19.5 to the dollar. The hardware is readily available and is not the hard part. The hard part is people trained and data collected; the models can be trained on rented machines for as long as needed.

What we suggest. Start with A, because it tests the only things that matter first: whether people keep to the picture protocol, whether the pairs come in, and whether a first model checks out against the scale. Move to B when the pairs are worth more than the people can take by hand, and to C when a district or a ministry wants the data to stay with it and will own the machine. Hardware can be bought in a week at any point; a trained meat inspector and a cycle of pairs cannot. Rented GPUs carry the training for as long as needed; a machine of our own, or the province's, is a decision about ownership and sovereignty, not about speed.

Ownership. Before the first model is trained, three things must be settled in writing. The records are held and owned by the system's operator under terms of service that the cattle owner accepts at registration; the owner has access to their own animals' records, the right to correct them, and a say over who sees them beyond the system's stated uses. Ownership by each cattle owner would make every later use a negotiation and is not proposed. The aggregate (counts, coverage, movements, clusters) goes to the ministry, and tracking sick animals, movements and outbreaks is never conditional on an owner's consent: that is animal health, but a record that names an owner, her number and her kraal is personal data under the Data Protection Act 2021, and is handled as such. The models belong to whoever is set up to hold them. One structure worth considering is a special purpose vehicle in which the people who supply the data (farmers through their cooperatives, the feedlot, the abattoir), the partners who build, and government hold the models and the aggregate together, so that none of them can take the whole, and a change of project or of supplier does not take the models with it. Open: the form, the shares, who chairs it.

6. Unresolved questions, with the options

  1. Identity: muzzle, tag, or both. The Animal Identification Act requires official tags, and the mandated system has reached a tenth of its target. Muzzle matching reads the nose ridge pattern, independent of the coat, so uniform breeds are not the problem; wet or muddy noses, light, movement and growing calves are. Options: tag as the identity and muzzle as a check against tag loss and fraud; or, where tags have not reached, the muzzle as a provisional local record, never a substitute for the official identity. Test: thirty to a hundred known animals, photographed twice a week apart, matched on our phones. Kill test for the muzzle route.
  2. Where the record is written. Dip tank, sale pen, feedlot gate, abattoir, vet camp, checkpoint, milk centre. Never one place only. Open: which places have a person and a phone every time an animal passes.
  3. GPS on some animals in large herds. A collar or ear-tag with GPS on a few animals per herd would give movement without a person writing it. Open: cost per unit, battery, theft, and whether a few animals stand for the herd. Option: start without, with the place of every entry (the dip tank's GPS) as the movement record, and add collars in big herds and near borders later.
  4. The herder's phone as the sensor. The phone's GPS at each entry gives place for free; a background track would give the herd's range. Open: consent, battery, coverage, and whether the herder's phone is his own. Option: GPS only at the moment of an entry, never a track, until someone asks for one.
  5. WhatsApp, a dedicated chat app, or screens. WhatsApp is proven and natural but runs over a foreign company's servers. If that is not wanted, for data security or sovereignty, the same chat is built as a dedicated app on servers in Zambia, at the cost of getting it installed. Screens with icons suit the intake and the record views. Likely all three, by place and by role. One more fact for the choice: the Tonga translator sits on NLLB, Meta's open model, and the line runs on Meta's WhatsApp platform, so the project already builds on two things Meta gives away; government itself works on Facebook daily. Whether Meta would support the project in some form is an open question worth asking, with the dedicated-app route kept as the exit.
  6. Feature phones. SMS and USSD cover them for reminders and one-line reports, not for pictures. Open: the share of herders with a smartphone in the pilot area. Where there is a smartphone, WhatsApp is one way in that needs no install.
  7. Cameras in the field. Power (solar), connectivity (4G, store-and-forward, or a satellite terminal where there is no signal), weather, theft, and the dip tank committee's welcome. A community asset can be secured as communities secure the dip tank itself: custody, a keeper, a locked mount, a remotely disabled terminal. Option: a phone on a pole before any camera.
  8. Whose data. The system's, under terms of service accepted at registration (in Tonga, read aloud where needed); the owner sees and corrects their own animals' records and has a say over sharing beyond the stated uses; the ministry sees counts and movements. The Data Protection Act applies to the personal data in the records, names and numbers, whatever the terms say. It does not stop a sick animal, a movement or an outbreak being tracked: the animal record carries personal data wherever it identifies a person, and the Act applies to it, and reporting notifiable disease is a duty under the Animal Health Act in any case. The two laws sit side by side; the terms of service say which person sees which record, not whether the disease is followed. Open: the terms themselves, and how a herder accepts them for an owner who lives elsewhere.
  9. The mandated system. Who runs it, what it records, whether it exposes an interface. Option: write our record under it, never beside it. Ask the veterinary department early; this decides the export value.
  10. Who pays after a pilot. Government, buyers, lenders, insurers, cooperatives, farmers. None proven. Option: the cooperative as the first payer for the price and dip modules, the abattoir for the carcass link.
  11. Vets and vaccines. If the shortage is vets, vaccines or dip chemicals, information alone moves little. Test early with one camp officer and one vet.
  12. Use without chasing. Smallholder apps die with their projects. The only verdict is herders who keep using it. WhatsApp as a way in is one answer: no install, no new habit. Test: ten herders shown the mockup and the WhatsApp way side by side.
  13. The abattoir's willingness. Carcass data is theirs; the link needs their yes and their grading convention. One abattoir in Choma is enough to start.
  14. Who owns the models. The records are the operator's under the terms of service, the aggregate the ministry's; the models need a holder. Option: a special purpose vehicle of the data suppliers, the builders and government (5b).

7. What is not needed first

Muzzle identification, drone or fixed-camera views, weight from a photo, breeding models, credit. All of them depend on the pairs that the first cycle produces. The first build is the record store, the intake form, the sick report and reply, and the movement entry.

8. Sequence, as a list

  1. Partners: one feedlot, one abattoir, one vet, one dip tank committee. Ask the veterinary department.
  2. Protocol: the standard picture sheet; the intake form; consent in Tonga.
  3. Build: record store, intake, sick report, movement entry. Tonga labels from speakers.
  4. Train the people: the person at the crush, the abattoir staff and the meat inspectors, on the standard picture and the entry, an afternoon each.
  5. Collect: one feedlot cycle of pairs; the muzzle test; a season of sick reports and dips.
  6. Check: the first weight model against the scale, on animals it has never seen.
  7. Decide, with the numbers, what phase two is.
